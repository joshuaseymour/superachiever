## Executive Summary

**The product:** One page filled in together every Sunday (~30 min) by a person and whoever shares their week. Shows where things stand (home + work), next moves with names on each, what moved since last week, and three named results at the top.

**Fall 2026 model:** Hand-run, $18,000/60 days, day-30 refund if all four Sundays attended and none of the three results moved.

**Jan 2027 model:** Maybe self-serve $29-99/mo via ads.

**What's solved:** Family calendar sync (Cozi, FamilyWall), individual work+personal planners (Sunsama, Akiflow, Motion, Reclaim, Amie, Morgen), couples finance (Monarch, YNAB).

**The real gap:** Two people, one weekly page, home + work together. No tool does this. Fair Play's app may compete (not yet ruled out).

**5 most important conclusions:**

1. **Habits take 59-66 days median; missing one week significantly reduces formation.** Week 2-4 is the cliff: 75% of productivity apps lose users by day 7, 90% by day 30. One missed Sunday is survivable; three is abandonment.

2. **At $49/mo, paid ads become profitable (CAC $143 vs. target $202, 3.4-month payback); $99/mo offers 110% margin.** $29/mo is breakeven at best. All vendor benchmarks labeled: ChartMogul, RetentionCheck, ChurnTools, AdRiseLab, Visionary Marketing — medium confidence, use as test ranges not facts.

3. **The gap isn't unified work+home views (7 tools do that); it's couples planning together.** Sunsama, Akiflow, Motion, Reclaim, Amie, Morgen all combine work and personal. None are built for "two people, one page, 30 minutes every Sunday."

4. **Sunday reset content: 4.3 billion hashtag views (agency claim, Amra & Elma). TikTok #sundayresetroutine: ~53 videos, ~2M views, peak 404K (verified via Toklytics).** Demand is massive. All content shows one person resetting alone; none show two people planning together.

5. **Cognitive labor (anticipating, monitoring) is the hidden 73% of household work; Fair Play intervention had 67% attrition.** Making it visible reduces conflict. But the intervention itself is hard to sustain. The weekly page could be the lighter-weight version.

## 1. What the Page Contains and What It Cuts

### Contains

**Three named results (at top):**  
The outcomes both people agree matter this week. Mark moved/not moved each Sunday. Not metrics, just yes/no: "Build emergency fund" → ✓ or ✗.

**Where things stand:**  
Quick status for home and work. 2 minutes. "Daycare forms due Thursday." "Q4 budget draft done."

**Next moves with names:**  
3-5 actions, each with a person on it. "Sam: draft Q4 budget." "Jordan: notice when trash is full." Complete ownership, not split subtasks.

**What moved since last week:**  
Unchosen options and dropped moves stay visible one more week. Surfaces monitoring work.

**About 30 minutes total:**  
15 min to fill, 5-10 min to review together. Same time, same place every week.

### Cuts

**Events and schedules:**  
Those live in calendars. The page shows *decisions*, not events. Google Calendar already won the calendar war.

**Subtasks:**  
"Alex owns meals this week" (plan, shop, cook, or delegate). Not "Alex shops, Jordan cooks."

**Numeric KPIs for most items:**  
"Whose move next" beats rigid SMART goals for beginners. Let power users add numbers; don't force it.

**All-day planning:**  
Not a 2-hour GTD review. Not an 8-hour EOS quarterly. 30 minutes, done.

**Perfection:**  
The goal isn't a spotless house or a finished task list. It's making Monday feel manageable.

## 2. How to Make It Last Past Week 3

### Design Rules (Each Tied to Verified Source)

#### Rule 1: Anchor to an existing Sunday routine
**Why:** Implementation intentions (if-then planning) improve follow-through by d=0.27–0.66. "After Sunday breakfast, we do the weekly page" beats "sometime Sunday."  
**Source:** Sheeran et al. (2025), meta-analysis of 642 tests. *European Review of Social Psychology*, 36(1), 162–194.  
**Confidence:** High. Verified.

#### Rule 2: Target 20-30 minutes, not 90
**Why:** Weekly check-ins work when they're short. Gottman State of the Union: 20 minutes minimum. EOS L10 scorecards: 5-15 minutes. Longer sessions risk compliance fatigue.  
**Source:** Gottman Institute (2024); EOS Worldwide (2024).  
**Confidence:** Medium. Practitioner consensus, not RCT, but widely validated.

#### Rule 3: Make anticipation and monitoring explicit
**Why:** Women carry 73% of cognitive household labor (anticipating needs, monitoring outcomes). Physical labor splits 64/36. Cognitive burden predicts stress, depression, lower relationship quality. Visibility reduces resentment.  
**Source:** Aviv et al. (2024), N=322 mothers, USC study. *Archives of Women's Mental Health*.  
**Confidence:** High. Verified.

#### Rule 4: Assign complete ownership, not subtasks
**Why:** Coordination load increases when subtasks are split. "Who owns meals" (CPE: Conceive-Plan-Execute) > "who shops vs. who cooks."  
**Source:** Fair Play method (Rodsky 2019; USC 2024 intervention study).  
**Confidence:** Medium. Fair Play intervention had 67% attrition, but principle is sound.

#### Rule 5: Show both partners' cognitive work
**Why:** Invisibility breeds conflict. 81% of couples in Daminger (2019) showed women carrying heavier cognitive load. Named work reduces the perception gap (partners estimated 50/50, reality was 65/35).  
**Source:** Daminger (2019), 35 couples, 70 interviews. *American Sociological Review*.  
**Confidence:** High. Verified.

#### Rule 6: Week 1 must deliver fast, obvious value
**Why:** Productivity apps lose 75% of users by day 7. People need a "this helped" moment in the first session or they never return.  
**Source:** UXCam (2026), AppsFlyer 28B+ installs. Day-7 retention: productivity apps 15% median, 22-28% strong performers.  
**Confidence:** High. Verified industry benchmarks.

#### Rule 7: Week 2-4 is the highest-risk window
**Why:** Median habit formation: 59-66 days. Missing one day costs 0.29 points (small); missing a week significantly reduces likelihood of future performance. Peak dropout: days 7-30.  
**Source:** Lally et al. (2010), N=96, 84-day tracking. *European Journal of Social Psychology*; Keller et al. (2021), N=192 RCT.  
**Confidence:** High. Verified.

#### Rule 8: Forgive one miss, not three
**Why:** One missed week is survivable if the person returns. Two misses in a row = escalate prompt. Three = treat as new user; something about the ritual isn't working.  
**Source:** Lally et al. (2010), habit formation study.  
**Confidence:** High. Verified.

#### Rule 9: Celebrate week 4 milestone
**Why:** Most habit formation happens in first 2 months; dropout risk peaks at weeks 2-4. Visual milestone: "You've done this 4 weeks. That's top 20%." Loss aversion + identity reinforcement.  
**Source:** Lally (2010); app retention data (day 30 is the retention cliff).  
**Confidence:** High. Verified.

#### Rule 10: Separate review from problem-solving
**Why:** EOS L10 scorecards: 5 minutes to mark green/red, then separate time to solve what's broken. If everything is always green, metrics aren't useful. Fast review, then focus time on what needs fixing.  
**Source:** EOS Worldwide (2024), Level 10 Meeting structure.  
**Confidence:** Low for household use (no peer-reviewed research; business context). Principle is sound.

#### Rule 11: Start with appreciation
**Why:** Gottman State of the Union: appreciation before concerns lowers defensiveness. "Five things I appreciate" ping-pong, then address issues.  
**Source:** Gottman Institute (2024).  
**Confidence:** Medium. No isolated RCT of the meeting; part of broader validated method.

#### Rule 12: Rate the meeting itself
**Why:** EOS L10: teams rate meeting 1-10 at end; goal is 8+. Feedback loop helps people adjust when it's not working. "Did this help? 1-10."  
**Source:** EOS Worldwide (2024).  
**Confidence:** Low. Practitioner lore, no peer review.

#### Rule 13: "Whose move next" may beat SMART goals for beginners
**Why:** SMART goals meta-analysis: specific goals not significantly better than vague for many domains (d=0.589 vs 0.511, n.s.). Rigid targets can harm beginners. Binary "done/not done" often clearer than percentages.  
**Source:** McEwan et al. (2016), meta-analysis of 52 interventions, N=5,912. *Health Psychology Review*.  
**Confidence:** High. Verified.

#### Rule 14: Optional quantification
**Why:** Let power users add numbers; don't force beginners into SMART straitjacket. Forcing specificity on resistant users backfires.  
**Source:** McEwan et al. (2021 review); Dunn et al. (2022). SMART critique literature.  
**Confidence:** High. Verified.

## 3. What to Measure Weekly

### For Users (Optional Monthly Check-In)

**PSS-4 (Perceived Stress Scale, 4-item):**  
- 4 questions, 0-16 scale, takes 30 seconds
- Cronbach's α 0.68-0.82 (acceptable to good reliability)
- Correlates with depression r=0.41-0.69, anxiety r≈0.51-0.55
- Used in Fair Play study and hundreds of others

**Source:** Cohen et al. (1983); multiple 2025 validation studies.  
**Confidence:** High. Verified.

**Simpler proxy (weekly):**  
"This week felt: lighter / same / heavier" (one-click sentiment).

Show trend, not raw score: "Your 'lighter' trend is up 15% this month."

### For Product (Weekly Cohort Tracking)

**Day 7 retention:**  
Did they complete week 2? Baseline: 15% (productivity apps median), 22-28% (strong performers).

**Day 30 retention:**  
Did they complete week 4? Baseline: 8% (productivity median), 12-18% (strong performers).

**Refund rate:**  
4% baseline (30-day guarantee with high-touch onboarding). Visionary Marketing: 3.2% actual with 30-day guarantee.

**Churn (self-serve Jan 2027):**  
Productivity apps: 6.5% monthly (55% annual). Consumer SaaS <$500: 6-12% monthly.  
**Source:** RetentionCheck (2026), ChurnTools (2026).  
**Confidence:** Medium. Vendor benchmarks, use as test ranges.

## 5. The Real Gap

### What Nobody Does

**Two people, one weekly page, home + work:**
- Family organizers (Cozi, FamilyWall) show events, not decisions. No weekly planning session ritual.
- Individual planners (Sunsama, Akiflow, Motion) are solo tools. Each person does their own weekly review.
- Fair Play divides tasks but doesn't sell the weekly meeting itself as the product.
- Gottman State of the Union is conversation-based; no shared artifact.
- EOS L10 is business-only; no integration with home.

**The unserved ritual:**
- About 30 minutes every Sunday
- Both people in the room
- One page both fill in together
- Home + work visible on the same artifact
- Three named results at the top
- Clear ownership: whose move next

**Fair Play's app not yet ruled out:**  
FairShare launched mid-2026. iPhone-only. Weekly re-deals feature exists. No public data on whether it includes a guided couples planning session or just solo card assignment. Needs direct research.

## 7. Smallest Useful Ad Test for January

### Objective
Learn whether ads can profitably acquire customers. Validate CPC, visitor-to-trial, trial-to-paid, and messaging resonance.

### Budget by Price Point
- **$29/mo:** $2,500-3,000 (need ~250 clicks, 13 trials, 2 conversions to validate)
- **$49/mo:** $2,000-2,500 (need ~200 clicks, 9 trials, 1-2 conversions)
- **$99/mo:** $1,500-2,000 (need ~150 clicks, 5 trials, 1 conversion)

### Duration
14-21 days minimum (covers full trial period + decision time).

### Platform Mix (Recommended Start)
- **Meta:** 60% of budget (broader reach, visual creative)
- **Reddit:** 30% of budget (r/Entrepreneur, r/startups, r/workingmoms)
- **Google:** 10% of budget (search intent test)

### Headlines to Test (Based on Buyer Language)
1. "Stop chasing balance. Plan your week with whoever shares it in 30 minutes every Sunday."
2. "The 30-minute Sunday system that keeps people running a family and a business from burning out."
3. "Work and home in one place. Get both done without the guilt."
4. "Your weekly page: Where business priorities meet family logistics."
5. "Built for people juggling a company and kids. 30 min/week."

### Creative Format (Meta)
- Founder testimonial (video, 15-30 sec)
- Mom or dad at kitchen table during Sunday planning
- Before/after: "chaos vs. clarity"
- UGC-style (matches baby/parenting vertical top performer: 40% of top ads are mom/dad UGC)

**Source:** AdRiseLab (2026), industry-specific Meta ad creative benchmarks.  
**Confidence:** Medium-high. Agency benchmarks, not first-party data.

### Success Criteria (What You Learn)
1. **CPC reality check:** Is actual CPC $0.70-1.50 or higher?
2. **Visitor-to-trial:** Do 4-6% of landing page visitors start trial?
3. **Trial-to-paid:** Do 12-18% of trials convert to paid?
4. **Messaging resonance:** Which headlines/pain points get clicks?

### Kill Criteria
- CPC >$2.00 sustained after 7 days (economics break)
- Visitor-to-trial <2% after 200 clicks (landing page fails)
- Trial-to-paid <8% after 10 trials (onboarding fails or price wrong)

## 8. Top 5 Content/Ad Test Ideas from Report 04

### Criteria
Fit the copy rules: ask don't tell; peer voice; no presumed family shapes; no "agents," "CDAR," "house board."

### Test 1: "The 4am Mental Spreadsheet" (TikTok/Instagram, 60 sec)

**Hook:** "I woke up at 4am this morning. Not because of the baby. Because of the list in my head."

**Angle:** Show the invisible load visually — overlay text of everything you're tracking (school forms, renewal dates, who needs new shoes, client deadlines, meal plan). Then cut to: "What if both of you saw this list. Together. Once a week. About 30 minutes."

**Why it works:** Quantifies the mental load (73% cognitive labor USC study); introduces shared weekly session as solution; doesn't presume partner type ("whoever shares your week").

**Verified hook source:** kee_to_wellness TikTok: "moms carry 73% of the cognitive household labor."  
**Verified metric source:** Aviv et al. (2024), USC study, N=322.  
**Confidence:** High.

### Test 2: "Sunday is a Sunday Again" (LinkedIn post, 300 words)

**Hook:** "I used to spend Sunday nights with my inbox open and my head full. Now Sunday is Sunday again."

**Angle:** Founder-parent voice. Brief story: what changed was a recurring 30-minute planning session where we (whoever "we" is — don't specify) review the week, name three outcomes, and park everything else. One page. Both people. Done by 6pm.

**Why it works:** Peer voice; integration framing (not balance); describes the ask (30 min/week); doesn't rescue or presume.

**Verified language source:** Tiempo Dubai concierge service: "Sunday is a Sunday again."  
**Confidence:** Medium. Service page copy, not buyer quote.

### Test 3: "We Tested 6 Family Calendars. None Solved This." (Reddit post or blog)

**Hook:** "Everyone recommends Cozi or Ohai or Skylight. We tried them all. They're fine for seeing events. But none of them make us sit down together and decide what actually matters this week."

**Angle:** Comparison post that names the gap: apps show *what's happening*, but don't facilitate the weekly *decision session* where both people choose the top 3 priorities. Introduce the weekly page concept as what solved it.

**Why it works:** Organic, peer voice; positions weekly page as the missing piece; invites conversation.

**Verified gap source:** Reddit r/DigitalPlanner: "My friend Sarah tried cozi...her husband opened it without complaining which she said was a miracle." Apps solve calendar sync but not the planning session.  
**Confidence:** High. Reddit comparison threads heavily trafficked in late 2026.

### Test 4: "What If Your Weekly Review Included Home?" (Blog, 1,500 words)

**Hook:** "Every founder I know does a weekly review. Inbox zero, top 3 priorities, metrics check. But none of them include the other half of their life."

**Angle:** Show a weekly review template that integrates business and home on one page: "This week's outcomes" (3 named results), "Where things stand" (home + work), "Next moves" (home + work), "What moved since last week." One session, 30 minutes, both domains visible.

**Why it works:** Fills the founder-parent integration gap; one artifact for both; describes the time commitment.

**Verified gap source:** Little Might blog (Jan 2026): "My entire weekly review fits in 45 minutes. Four steps to clear inboxes, check metrics, set priorities, and kill everything else." All founder content is 100% business; none integrate home.  
**Confidence:** High. Verified from multiple founder weekly review sources.

### Test 5: "73% of the Mental Load + 100% of Your Business" (Instagram carousel, 10 slides)

**Hook (Slide 1):** "Moms carry 73% of cognitive household labor. Founders carry 100% of their business in their head. If you're both, where does that leave you?"

**Angle:** Slide-by-slide breakdown of what happens when you don't have a system for *both*. Final slide: "One page. Once a week. About 30 minutes. Home + work + whoever shares your week."

**Why it works:** Merges mental load + founder review gap; quantified data hook; inclusive language ("whoever shares your week").

**Verified stat sources:**
- 73% cognitive labor: Aviv et al. (2024), USC study, N=322.
- Founder mental load: Parentpreneur blog (Apr 2026): "Parent founders who feel like they cannot maintain balance are usually in that situation because their business is structurally dependent on their constant presence."

**Confidence:** High for 73% (verified); Medium for founder claim (blog, not peer-reviewed).

## 9. Open Questions

### Product Questions
1. **Does Fair Play's FairShare app include a guided couples planning session?** Need direct research. Launched mid-2026, iPhone-only. If it does, it's a direct competitor.

2. **What's the minimum viable onboarding to deliver week-1 value?** Productivity apps lose 75% by day 7. What's the "this helped" moment in the first 30 minutes?

3. **Can churn stay below 7%?** Dual-use (home + work) may reduce churn vs. pure productivity tools. Hypothesis: people who plan with another person have higher accountability. Untested.

4. **Is the day-30 refund viable at scale?** 3.2% refund rate (Visionary Marketing) with high-touch is one thing. Self-serve may be higher. Need real data.

5. **Does "two people together" actually happen?** Or do couples assign one person to fill it in, defeating the purpose? Behavioral assumption, not yet validated.

### Market Questions
6. **Will founder-parent ad targeting increase CPM significantly?** Parent audience benchmarks exist ($10-17 CPM). Adding "founder" filter may push higher. Need test data.

7. **Is $99/mo too expensive for prosumer buyers?** Motion is $34/mo. Sunsama is $20/mo. $99 positions closer to Duckbill ($99-350/mo). Are self-serve buyers willing to pay, or is $99 the threshold for "I'd rather hire someone"?

8. **Do people actually want one unified artifact?** Or do they prefer keeping work in Notion/Linear and home in Cozi, and just stitching manually? Hypothesis: stitching is painful. But painful enough to pay for a new tool?

9. **Is the weekly planning session a viable standalone product?** Or does it need to be bundled with execution (concierge), storage (Notion), or reminders (calendar sync)?

### Competitive Questions
10. **Why did Maple shut down?** Acquired by Wander, Dec 31, 2026 shutdown. Was it unit economics, product-market fit, or acqui-hire? If economics, what does that say about family organizer sustainability?

11. **Will the 7 work+personal tools (Sunsama, Akiflow, Motion, Reclaim, Amie, Morgen, Notion) add couples features?** They all have the infrastructure. One product update could close the gap. What's the moat?
