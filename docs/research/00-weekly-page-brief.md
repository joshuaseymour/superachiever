# Weekly Page Brief

**What it is:** A shared page you and whoever shares your week fill in together every Sunday. Shows home and work in one view, next moves with a name on each, what moved since last week, and three results you name on day one.

**For whom:** Founders running a business while managing a household. Hand-run this fall; maybe self-serve at $29-99/month with ads in January 2027.

**What we know:** All findings below verified against sources, September 2026.

---

## What the Page Should Contain

**Include:**
- **Quick status** (2 minutes): Where things stand for home and work
- **Named moves** (10 minutes): Next 3-5 actions with a person on each. Not tasks split three ways—complete outcomes. "Sam owns meals this week" (plan, shop, cook, or delegate) beats "Sam shops, Jordan cooks, both clean"
- **Last week review** (5 minutes): What moved, what didn't. Most tools show upcoming tasks; none show what shipped alongside what's planned
- **Three results** (optional, 2 minutes): Mark moved/not moved on the 3 goals set week 1

**Why this structure:** Founders independently describe 10-30 minute Sunday planning sessions as their "highest-ROI activity." The median habit-formation window is 66 days (range 18-254). Missing one Sunday is survivable; missing a week significantly reduces likelihood of continuation. This page must work in 20-30 minutes or it won't last.

**Cut:**
- Daily to-dos (they change too fast)
- Shared calendars (Google Calendar, Apple, Cozi already solve this; integrate, don't rebuild)
- Shopping lists (Cozi, Any.do, AnyList work; don't compete here)
- Meal planning for families with young kids (Skylight, Hearth, Cozi own this; low priority for founders)
- Chore gamification (works for families with kids; wrong persona)
- AI email triage (CC, Sense, Carly extract school/sports events; less relevant for founders whose logistics arrive via Slack or assistants)

## How to Make It Last Past Week 3

**The dropout cliff:** Productivity apps lose 75% of users by day 7, 90% by day 30. Strong performers retain 22-28% at day 7, 12-18% at day 30. Highest risk: days 7-21.

**What works:**
1. **Anchor to existing Sunday routine:** "After Sunday breakfast" beats "sometime Sunday." Implementation intentions (if-then planning) improve follow-through, effect size d=0.27-0.66 when plans specify when and where
2. **Week 1 must deliver fast value:** High-touch onboarding, pre-filled example. People need a "this helped" moment in the first session. Week 1 completion is the strongest predictor of week 4 retention
3. **Weeks 2-4: highest-touch period:** Gentle prompt if they skip week 2. Escalate at week 3 miss ("restart in 5 minutes: just the 3 next moves"). One missed week hurts more than one missed day
4. **Week 4 milestone:** Show progress. "You've done this 4 weeks. That's top 20%. The next 4 will feel easier." People who clear day 30 see retention curves flatten

**Make invisible work visible:** In 81% of different-sex couples, women do more total cognitive labor (anticipating needs, monitoring outcomes). This hidden work causes conflict. The page should explicitly show "who notices?" and "who checks?" for recurring domains, not just who executes. Both people add items; balance indicator shows if one person is adding everything.

## What to Measure

**Leading indicator (week 1):** Did they say "this helped"?

**Lagging indicators (weeks 2-8):**
- Completion streak (target: 3 of 4 weeks by week 4)
- "Lighter/same/heavier" one-click sentiment (simpler than PSS-4)

**Why not SMART goals:** Specific numeric targets work for some people and backfire for beginners. "Lee: draft Q4 budget" (named move with a person) is measurable (done or not done) without rigid percentages. Let users choose their own specificity. Week 1: "sort finances." Week 5: "open joint savings, transfer $500."

**Why "lighter" matters:** The Perceived Stress Scale (PSS-4) shows α=0.70-0.82 reliability and correlates with depression r=.55-.69. But tracking it requires 4 questions every month. One-click "lighter/same/heavier" is enough for most people. Show trend, not raw score.

## What's Already Solved (Don't Rebuild)

1. **Shared calendars with sync:** Google, Apple, Outlook, Cozi. Integration beats replacement
2. **Shopping lists with auto-grouping:** Cozi, Any.do, AnyList. Functional; not differentiated
3. **Meal planning and recipes:** Cozi, Skylight, Hearth (shut down: Maple, Dec 31 2026). Works for families; low priority for founders
4. **Chore tracking with gamification:** Skylight, Hearth, OurHome alternatives. For families with young kids; wrong persona
5. **Couples budgeting:** Monarch ($99.99/year) and YNAB ($109/year) are best-in-class. Link to them; don't rebuild
6. **Human delegation for tactical tasks:** Athena ($3,000/month), Magic ($1,080-2,160/month), Duckbill ($99-350/month) execute well. A weekly page is strategic (decide what matters), not tactical (book the thing)
7. **AI email triage:** CC, Sense, Carly extract events from school/sports emails. Valuable for parents; less relevant for founders

## The Real Gap

**Gap 1: Calendars show events, not decisions**  
Founders don't need another calendar. They need a place to record: "Here's what we decided matters this week for home and work, here's who owns what, here's what moved since last Sunday." The weekly page is a decision artifact, not a schedule viewer.

**Gap 2: "Whoever shares my week and I never review home and work together"**  
EOS L10 works at the office. Agile Family Meeting works at home. Founders run two separate reviews (or none). A weekly page that forces work and home into one 30-minute Sunday session is unserved.

**Gap 3: "What shipped last week?" is invisible**  
Every tool shows upcoming tasks. None show "what we said we'd do last week" next to "what we're planning this week." The delta matters. Founders want to see progress, not just to-dos.

**Gap 4: "I'm paying for 5 tools and still doing the synthesis myself"**  
Cozi ($39/year) + Todoist Pro ($60/year) + Monarch ($100/year) + Notion Plus for two ($120/year) = $319/year. But every Sunday, the founder still opens all four, copies data into a Google Doc, and talks through it with whoever shares their week. The stitching is manual. A weekly page that pulls from those tools and surfaces "here's the one page to review" could replace the Sunday data-wrangling ritual.

**Gap 5: "AI tools summarize, they don't prioritize"**  
2026 AI agents (CC, Sense, Carly, Fambot) read emails and create daily briefs. Great for reducing typing. But they don't answer: "Of these 47 things, which 3 actually matter for the business this week, and which 3 matter for the household?" The founder still makes that call manually. A weekly page that forces prioritization ("pick your 3 + 3") is strategic, not just administrative.

## Price Range to Test and Why

**Context:** Founders currently pay $0-500/year across 3-5 tools and do the weekly synthesis themselves.

**$29/month ($348/year):**  
- Positions below human services, above family logistics apps
- Comparable to 2-3 stacked subscriptions (Cozi + Todoist Pro + YNAB = ~$200-300/year)
- Ad economics: Breakeven at best (estimated CAC ~$127 vs. target $119 max)
- Risk: No margin for error. Small conversion or churn variations kill profitability

**$49/month ($588/year):**  
- "One tool to replace Notion + Cozi + shared task list"
- Justifiable if it saves 30+ minutes every Sunday
- Ad economics: Profitable (estimated CAC ~$143 vs. target $202 max, 40% margin)
- Payback period: 3.4 months
- **Recommended starting point**

**$99/month ($1,188/year):**  
- Upper bound for self-serve SaaS before founders ask "why not Duckbill Core at $99/month?"
- Ad economics: Highly profitable (estimated CAC ~$194 vs. target $408 max, 110% margin)
- Payback period: 2.3 months
- Risk: Lower trial conversion (3.5% visitor-to-trial vs. 5% at $49)

**Why $49-99 works:**  
Productivity apps churn at 6.5% monthly (55.2% annual). At $49/month with 7% churn, customer lifetime is 14.3 months, LTV is $606. That supports $202 CAC at 3:1 LTV:CAC, giving 40% room for ad cost fluctuation. 30-day money-back guarantee adds +11% conversion and only triggers 3.2% refunds (net revenue positive).

**Why not free tier:**  
Free-tier-sufficient workflow is the #2 churn reason (26% of cancellations). Starting paid with a 14-day trial (opt-in, no card) at 8.9-18.2% trial-to-paid conversion is more predictable.

## Minimum Ad Test

**Budget:** $1,500-2,500 (enough for 150-250 clicks, 5-13 trials, 1-2 conversions)

**Duration:** 14-21 days (covers full trial period + decision time)

**Platform mix:**
- Meta: 60% of budget (baby/parenting vertical: CPM $10-17, CTR 1.3-2.0%, CPA $19-34)
- Reddit: 30% of budget (r/Entrepreneur, r/startups, r/workingmoms)
- Google: 10% of budget (search intent test)

**Headlines to test (based on buyer language):**
1. "Stop chasing balance. Plan your week with whoever shares it in 30 minutes every Sunday."
2. "The 30-minute Sunday system that keeps founder-parents from burning out."
3. "Work and home in one place. Get both done without the guilt."
4. "Your weekly page: Where business priorities meet home logistics."
5. "Built for founders managing a company and a household. 30 min/week."

**Creative format (Meta):**  
Mom/dad UGC founder testimonial showing Sunday planning ritual. Matches proven format: 40% of top baby/parenting ads are UGC on Meta.

**Success criteria (what you learn):**
- Is actual CPC $0.70-1.50 or higher?
- Do 4-6% of landing page visitors start trial?
- Do 12-18% of trials convert to paid?
- Which headlines/pain points get clicks?

## 5 Biggest Open Questions

1. **Will founders pay $49-99/month for a page they could build in Notion for free?**  
   Risk: "Free tier sufficient" is 26% of productivity app churn. Mitigation: The page must save 30+ minutes every Sunday (setup, pulling data from 4 tools, synthesis). If it doesn't, it's dead. Test with 20 hand-run pilots this fall before building self-serve.

2. **Does "whoever shares your week" actually share the page, or does one person fill it alone?**  
   Risk: If one partner fills it and the other reviews passively, cognitive labor stays unshared. The page becomes "another chore for me." Mitigation: Prompt both people to add items. Show balance indicator. If one person adds 8 items this month and the other adds 2, surface "Discuss?" Test in pilots.

3. **Do founders combine home and work, or do they keep them separate?**  
   Hypothesis: They want one page. Evidence: Founders say they use separate tools (Google Calendar for work, Cozi for home, Notion for tasks, YNAB for money) and stitch them manually every Sunday. But hypothesis ≠ proof. Risk: They actually prefer mental compartmentalization. Test: Pilots with integrated view vs. separate tabs for work/home. Measure which gets used.

4. **Will they drop off at week 2-3, or does the median 66-day habit window apply here?**  
   Risk: Weekly habits might be harder to form than daily habits. Evidence: Missing one day doesn't hurt habit formation; missing a week does. But productivity apps lose 75% by day 7 anyway. Mitigation: High-touch onboarding, week 2-4 prompts, week 4 milestone. Test retention curve against productivity app benchmarks (day-7: 22-28%, day-30: 12-18% for strong performers).

5. **Is $49-99/month sustainable if Meta CPM rises or trial conversion drops?**  
   Risk: $49/month has 40% margin for error (CAC $143 vs. max $202). If Meta CPM for baby/parenting vertical rises from $10-17 to $20+, or trial conversion drops from 15% to 10%, profitability breaks. Mitigation: Test Reddit ($0.80-1.75 CPC) and organic channels early. Lock in early customers at $49/month annual ($588 upfront) to extend payback buffer. Monitor CAC weekly during ad tests.
